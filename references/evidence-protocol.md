# Evidence protocol

## Scope and sources

Record collection date, geography, level, task family, and observation period. Prefer official labor statistics for occupation trends, primary employer descriptions for requirements, and primary business disclosures for funded demand. Use platform reports with their population, taxonomy, denominator, and methodology stated. Treat practitioner feedback as local evidence, not population truth.

Read sources before relying on them. A search snippet is a lead; if inaccessible, retain an indexed-only status and lower confidence. Never present an archived example as an inspected active opening. Job existence, expansion, scarcity, and individual fit require different support.

Identify source families: five articles repeating one recruiting report are one underlying source. Record publication date and the period described. Treat testimonials, promotional forecasts, and employer complaints as weaker than independently corroborated behavior.

## Posting collection and comparison

- Record employer, title, requisition ID where available, location, level, tasks, necessary/optional qualifications, application URL, first observed date, collection date, and access/status.
- Deduplicate across boards using employer requisition and description. Preserve legitimate separate requisitions and location/headcount differences. Flag uncertain duplicates rather than silently removing them.
- Distinguish new requisition, repost, replacement hire, evergreen talent pool, internship, and umbrella campaign when evidence permits; otherwise mark unknown.
- A sample of 30–50 selected postings can reveal requirements; it is not a census or a basis for national counts. State sampling method, inspected count, distinct employers, inclusion criteria, and coverage.
- Prefer within-market comparisons: same region, level, time, metric, and source coverage. Separate undergraduate, graduate, senior, and principal hiring.

## Trend rules

Compute ordinary growth only when metric, unit, geography, task definition, seniority, period length, coverage, and deduplication are comparable. Record base count and absolute change beside percentages. Do not compute finite percentage growth from zero. Do not compare a stock of active ads with a flow of new ads.

Check seasonality and low bases. Snapshots can improve a trend hypothesis but do not establish causality. If only current postings exist, do not invent history or extrapolate a future CAGR.

Keep employment growth, recruiting needs, unique advertisements, requested headcount, completed hires, revenue, shipments, and funding distinct. Check whether productivity, vendor platforms, offshoring, contractors, or existing employees absorb new tasks without equivalent hiring.

## Scarcity and qualified supply

Triangulate comparable-level wage changes, verified hiring difficulty, willingness to train adjacent candidates, actual entrant paths, and recurring task-specific gaps. Control role/location mix in salary comparisons. An advertised range, executive package, or greater share of senior jobs is not a wage trend.

Do not equate public applicant counts with qualified applicants or infer scarcity from long-lived ads. Distinguish scarce skills from poor conditions, low compensation, excessive requirements, frozen hiring, or a tiny employer market.

Map supply from current specialists, adjacent occupations, graduates, retraining, geographic mobility, and substitutes. Identify what slows supply and why this person can overcome it more readily. Treat course popularity, profile counts, and community membership as noisy proxies, not qualified workforce counts.

Establish why the same role benefits from integrating a proposed skill combination. Unrelated degrees or unusual combinations do not establish employer value.

## Preparation and timing

Map critical prerequisites rather than averaging keyword overlap. Include proof-building, recruiting filters, language, formal eligibility, required credentials, data/compute access, supervision, and relevant experience. Verify changing legal or immigration requirements with authoritative sources if material.

Distinguish verified barriers from unknowns. Omitted experience is not absent experience. Give preparation estimates as ranges with prerequisites and milestones; revise using actual progress. Compare preparation with demand-adoption and supply scenarios, not a precise unsupported date for the end of an opportunity.

## Decision uncertainty

Keep a claim ledger with support, counterevidence, uncertainties, and source lineage. A negative search is lack of observed evidence within a stated scope, not proof of no jobs. Do not cherry-pick one employer to characterize an entire field.

Keep market attractiveness separate from personal investment suitability, and evidence strength separate from direction. Strong evidence of unavailable resources can justify deprioritization; an exciting idea without evidence remains exploration.

Consider weaker adoption, faster entrant supply, standardization/automation, concentration in few firms, and a different preferred location. Explain what survives. Avoid uncalibrated confidence percentages and weighted scores.
